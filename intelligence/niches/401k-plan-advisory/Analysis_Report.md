# 401(k) Plan Advisory: Comprehensive 17-Part Deep Analysis

## Executive Summary
401(k) Plan Advisory is the "Blue Chip" of financial services B2B. It is characterized by long sales cycles (6-18 months), high barriers to entry (fiduciary liability), and immense lifetime value. The shift toward "Fiduciary 3(38)" status and "Financial Wellness" programs has created a massive opening for advisors to capture high-net-worth rollovers at the participant level.

## Part 1: Industry Economics and Business Model Analysis
| Metric | Typical Range | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Typical Project Value | $5,000 - $15,000 | Plan setup or intensive fee audit |
| Average AUM Fee | 0.25% - 0.75% | Compresses as plan assets exceed $20M |
| Customer LTV | $250,000 - $500,000+ | Assets are extremely "sticky" (7-10 yr retention) |
| Gross Margin | 50% - 70% | Higher for pure fiduciary, lower for TPA-hybrid |
| CAC (High-Touch) | $5,000 - $15,000 | Requires multiple stakeholder meetings |

**Financial Indicators of a Good Client:**
- **Participant Count:** Plans with 100+ participants require an audit, making them prime targets for "compliance-led" marketing.
- **Asset Concentration:** Look for companies in high-wage industries (Tech, Pharma, Engineering) where per-participant assets are high.

**Discovery Questions:**
- "When was the last time you ran a benchmarking study against your recordkeeper's 'revenue sharing' agreements?"
- "What is your current 'Participation Rate'? Is it below the industry average of 70%?"

## Part 2: Customer Journey and Buying Behavior
1. **Awareness:** Often triggered by a DOL audit alert, a high-fee complaint from an employee, or a company merger.
2. **Consideration:** Fear of fiduciary liability (ERISA).
3. **Decision:** Validation of "Fee Transparency" and "Service Model."

**Internal Approval:** HR Director (Initial) -> CFO (Financial) -> CEO/Investment Committee (Final).

## Part 3: Discovery Call Architecture
- **Opening:** "I was reviewing your Form 5500 filing and noticed your plan participation has dropped 15%. Usually, this triggers a DOL audit for 'Partial Plan Termination.' Are you prepared for that?"
- **Diagnosis:** "Who currently signs your Fiduciary Bond? Are you personally liable for the plan's investment failures?"
- **Future State:** "What would it mean for your HR team if we handled 100% of the participant education and investment oversight?"

## Part 4: Marketing Landscape
| Channel | Effectiveness | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Form 5500 Data | Extreme | The ultimate "Prospecting Map" |
| HR Events | High | Positioning as a benefit enhancer |
| LinkedIn | High | Targeted outreach to CFOs/HR Directors |
| Content (ERISA) | High | Compliance-led authority building |

**Common Agency Failure:** Selling "Better Returns." Returns are secondary; CFOs care about **Liability Reduction** and **Operational Efficiency.**

## Part 5: Credibility Building Without Portfolio
- **The "Audit Approach":** "We aren't here to manage your plan yet. We are here to audit your current advisor's 'Hidden Fees.' Here is our 12-point Fee Audit framework."

## Part 6: Regulatory Environment
| Body | Relevance |
| :--- | :--- |
| DOL (Dept of Labor) | Enforces ERISA. Main regulator for plan sponsors. |
| IRS | Tax compliance for plan contributions. |
| ERISA | The foundational law governing retirement plans. |

## Part 7: Industry Vocabulary and Cultural Fluency
- **Jargon:** 3(38) Fiduciary (Discretionary), 3(21) Fiduciary (Non-discretionary), TPA (Third Party Administrator), Safe Harbor, Matching.
- **Landmines:** Confusing a "Broker" with a "Fiduciary." Using "Stock Picker" terminology.

## Part 8: Objection Handling
- **"We like our current advisor":** "I respect that. Most people do. But does your advisor take 100% of the legal liability for the investment lineup in writing? If not, you are the one on the hook."

## Part 9: Competitive Dynamics
- **Incumbent (The 'Old Guard'):** Positioning against their lack of transparency and high fees.
- **Generalists:** Position against their lack of ERISA-specific expertise.

## Part 10: Pain Points
- "I'm worried about a DOL audit."
- "Our employees keep complaining about the high fees."
- "The recordkeeper's platform is a nightmare for HR."

## Part 11: Qualification Framework
- **Ideal:** 50-500 employees, $10M-$50M in plan assets, high-wage industry.
- **Problematic:** "Micro-plans" (<$1M) or high-turnover retail industries.

## Part 12: Technology Stack
- **Standard:** BrightScope, Larkspur-RiXtrema, Empower, Fidelity NetBenefits.

## Part 13: Proposal Architecture
- **Structure:** 1. Executive Summary, 2. Fee Audit Results, 3. Fiduciary Shield Plan, 4. Participant Wellness Map, 5. Next Steps.

## Part 14: Success Metrics
- **Primary:** "Plan Fee Savings" and "Participation Rate Increase."

## Part 15: Pricing & Negotiation
- **Value-Anchor:** "Our 0.10% fee reduction saves your employees $2M over the next 10 years. Our service pays for itself 5x over."

## Part 16: Network & Relationships
- **Connectors:** ERISA Attorneys, Payroll Providers (ADP/Paychex), Benefits Brokers.

## Part 17: Future Outlook
- **Trend:** SECURE Act 2.0. Mandating auto-enrollment and expanding "Long-term Part-time" eligibility. This creates a massive administrative burden that advisors must solve.

# ColdIQ Outreach Mapping: 401(k) Advisory

### Triggers (The Event)
1. **No Fidelity Bond Reported:** Found on Form 5500 (Automatic red flag).
2. **"Other" Assets > 5%:** Indicates hard-to-value or risky investments.
3. **New CFO Appointment:** Triggers a vendor review 90% of the time.

### Signals (The Intent)
- **Signal:** Compliance vulnerability.
- **Need:** Professional fiduciary oversight to mitigate personal liability.

### Angles (The Hook)
- **Angle:** "I saw on your latest Form 5500 that your plan has $5M in 'Other' assets but no fidelity bond coverage. This is a top-3 trigger for a DOL audit this year. We help CFOs secure their plans before the auditors arrive."

# 5 Obscure Lead Sources
1. **Form 5500 "No Fidelity Bond" Filings:** The most direct "compliance-fail" trigger.
2. **DOL Audit Alerts (Delinquent Contributions):** Publicly available data on late plan payments.
3. **Participant "Flip-Flop" (80-120 Rule):** Identifying plans near the 100-participant audit threshold.
4. **CEO/CFO "Transition" News:** New leaders looking for "quick wins" in cost-cutting.
5. **Rapid Hiring Signals:** Companies hiring 20+ people in a quarter need a "Scalable Benefit" strategy.
