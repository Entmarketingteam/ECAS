# Recovery Outreach Sequence
**Signal:** FDA Warning Letter / State Board Disciplinary Action (resolved)
**Trigger:** 30 days post-action date
**Campaign env var:** `SL_CAMPAIGN_RECOVERY`
**Framing:** Recovery and growth — never reference the specific violation or agency

---

## Email 1 — Day 1

**Subject:** after the inspection

Running a compounding pharmacy means operating under scrutiny that most businesses never face.

The documentation requirements, USP compliance standards, beyond-use dating protocols — the average prescriber has no idea what it takes to keep the dispensary running cleanly. Most pharmacies don't have the bandwidth to think about growth when they're focused on staying current.

The ones that do come through that kind of pressure tend to come out with something more valuable than before: a tighter operation, and a core group of prescribers who stuck around. Those are your advocates.

Now is typically when the best compounding pharmacies shift their attention to the prescriber side — not to replace what they have, but to expand from a position of strength. The compliance work is done. The processes are tighter. This is the moment to grow the referral base.

We work with compounding pharmacies specifically on prescriber outreach — finding the providers in your market who are already writing compounding scripts or have patients who'd benefit, and getting {{company_name}} in front of them before a competitor does.

## Email 2 — Day 5

**Subject:** the prescribers who stayed

Something I've noticed working with compounding pharmacies: the ones who go through a difficult operational period and come out the other side often have *stronger* prescriber relationships than they had going in.

Not more of them. Stronger ones.

The providers who stayed through that period did it because they trust you — the quality, the relationship, the reliability. That's not nothing. Those prescribers are your advocates. They'll refer colleagues. They'll defend the relationship when a sales rep shows up with a competing pharmacy pitch.

The question is whether you have a system to expand that network or whether growth is still mostly word-of-mouth and whoever calls first.

Here's a rough model for what a small push on the prescriber side looks like:

5 new prescribers × 5 scripts/month × $300 avg script value = **$1,800/month per prescriber added**

At 10 new prescribers over 6 months, that's $18,000/month in recurring revenue from a one-time outreach push.

## Sequence Notes

- **Do not** reference the FDA letter, state board action, warning letter number, or specific violation in any email
- **Do not** name the regulatory agency that issued the action
- Framing is entirely forward-looking: strength, growth, systematizing loyalty
- The Airtable record stores `action_type`, `source`, and `internal_notes` for context — this data is for internal prioritization only and never surfaces in copy
- If the contact replies to Email 1 or 2, remove from sequence immediately and route to manual follow-up
- Sequence is intentionally short (10 days) — this audience has low tolerance for prolonged outreach during a recovery period
